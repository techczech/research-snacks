# Task

Score the options below as possible first Research Snacks. The author wants to compare the length of professional preparation internationally, including Asian systems, and test whether familiar minima are necessities or conventions. This is scoping only. Do not write the snack or choose for the author. Score from these goals and verified feasibility, not the report writer's preference.

## Human signal

- Compare law, engineering and medicine across countries/regions, with Asian examples.
- Consider doctorates and education in general, but their inclusion is not fixed.
- Treat the conventional-minimum proposition as a hypothesis and consider contrary evidence.
- Compare matched endpoints, including supervised work and licensing.
- Return feasible shapes before author selection. No build or publication.

## Evidence available

UIS/World Bank and Eurydice describe education structures, not complete professional routes. WDOMS has programme duration but bulk access is paid/licensed. National professional stages require curation. No unified global route dataset was located. SED has strong US doctorate-duration data, but international clock compatibility remains unverified. Medicine has located studies comparing accelerated and conventional courses; selected early outcomes are similar and some exam results differ. Country-level routes alone do not identify the effect of years on competence.

## Subjects

### Timeline

An interactive stage timeline for medicine, law and engineering across six selected jurisdictions/systems, including Japan, India and Singapore. It separates prior degrees, professional education, paid supervised work, licensing and later specialist/title recognition. It offers clearly labelled school-entry, school-leaving and age-18 starting points, with doctorates separate. It shows institutional route variation, without identifying a causal optimum. Primary-source curation and endpoint matching are substantial. Estimated agent effort: two to four working days, with narrowing if endpoints fail.

### Evidence comparison

A comparison centred on accelerated and conventional medical education, with a smaller international route table. It separates common examinations, workplace assessments and patient outcomes, records selection and later training, and tests claims about the value of extra years in particular settings. It can engage the hypothesis directly, without establishing a universal minimum. It covers fewer professions and defers most doctorate questions. Estimated agent effort: one to two working days for full-paper appraisal and a small preview.

### Education explorer

A country explorer of school durations, bachelor's structures and formal doctoral routes from UIS/World Bank, Eurydice and selected national sources. It distinguishes legal requirements, scheduled study, attained education and actual completion. It can establish that familiar education structures are not universal, and indicate professional follow-up questions. It cannot establish independent-practice milestones or competence. Estimated agent effort: one to two working days for bounded coverage; international observed doctorate duration remains uncertain.

## Rubric

Use three equally weighted criteria, each from 1 to 10. Overall is the arithmetic mean. Anchors: 3 = serious mismatch or unresolved dependence on unavailable data; 6 = useful with substantial compromises; 9 = close fit with a feasible, clearly bounded result. Do not infer that source-existence checks verify all route values.

1. **Fit to the author's question.** Does the first output preserve international and Asian variation in professional preparation while addressing the conventional-minimum hypothesis?
2. **Strength of warranted inference.** Can the output answer what it advertises using the located evidence, with matched definitions and contrary findings visible?
3. **Feasibility of the first preview.** Is the route to a useful, source-traceable preview proportionate, with manageable access and curation gaps?

## Calibration

Before scoring, compare two claims: (i) different authorised pathways exist, with outcomes considered separately; (ii) a shorter national degree proves the extra years in every longer system are unnecessary. Confirm that (i) is better warranted by the packet. If not, explain the rubric failure rather than silently scoring.
