## Dead ends and interpretation changes

1. WDOMS turned out to be a paid licensed bulk source, not a ready open global dataset. Retain it as a candidate, but a small route audit can use regulators without buying the database.
2. Barro–Lee answers attained schooling, not required professional duration. Set it aside for the core comparison.
3. A ready internationally harmonised doctorate-duration table was not verified. OECD/HESA/Eurodoc/NISTEP cannot be promised as interchangeable SED substitutes.
4. US and international medical graduates have different pass rates, yet a patient-outcome study reports no corresponding international-graduate disadvantage on its mortality measure. These are distinct populations and endpoints, not a contradiction to average away.
5. The accelerated-route literature offers a more direct test of calendar duration. It also reports lower scores on some assessments, so a blanket claim of identical outcomes would omit evidence.
6. The EU directive search initially surfaced the original six-years-or-hours wording. A newer consolidation confirms five years AND 5,500 hours. The report uses the dated consolidation.
7. The public Washington Accord overview did not state one universal number of course years. Its equivalence purpose and the separate competence agreement are the defensible statements at this stage.

A further source-existence check opened [the UIS India mapping workbook](https://download.uis.unesco.org/ISCED%20mapping/ISCED_2011_Mapping_India.xlsx) in memory. It is an actual XLSX, with school-year reference 2020, programme names, minimum entry requirements, qualifications, theoretical entrance ages, theoretical durations and ISCED codes. The 2011 in the filename identifies the ISCED framework, not the source's observation year. The workbook groups MBBS and LLB in a single long-first-degree row with a duration range; that row cannot supply separate precise medical and legal qualification durations. This strengthens the data basis for the education-structure option while preserving the need for regulator documents in the professional-route option. The raw workbook was not saved or redistributed.

The SED 2025 source page was also checked directly for actual Excel links. One identified [duration-table download](https://ncses.nsf.gov/pubs/nsf26326/assets/data-tables/tables/nsf26326-tab001-012.xlsx) is a public aggregate table, not respondent-level microdata. World Bank duration indicators and Barro–Lee expose structured downloads; USMLE has actual aggregate counts/pass rates in HTML tables. OECD combines reports with statistical tables, but a suitable clean country-by-programme-duration extract remains unverified. WDOMS is a real database with licensed paid bulk access, not data currently held by this investigation. Eurydice and Eurodoc structure profiles, NCBE comparisons, national regulators and engineering standards are documentary sources from which a research table would need to be coded. HESA, NISTEP and OECD doctorate-career work remain unverified as sources for compatible elapsed-doctorate-duration data.

Proposed method 1 is a structured comparative document analysis: predefine jurisdictions, profession/specialty and endpoints; code one record per route and stage from regulators; preserve prerequisites, overlaps, minimum/typical distinctions, cohort/date and source passages; calculate only comparable required-route totals. Proposed method 2 is a focused evidence synthesis: prioritise direct shorter/longer training comparisons, extract study design and published effects/uncertainty, distinguish examinations from workplace/patient outcomes, record selection and overlapping samples, and pool only genuinely compatible outcomes if the evidence supports doing so. Existing papers do not supply an open individual-level dataset for rerunning their analyses; the 2026 intern study offers data on reasonable request. Proposed method 3 combines secondary-data analysis of World Bank/UIS and SED tables with harmonisation of UIS programme workbooks; preserve reference years, country coverage and nominal-versus-observed clocks, and keep attainment statistics separate from training requirements. These are proposed methods, not authorised empirical runs or completed analyses.

### EU Regulated Professions Database: actual duration data found

The [Commission database](https://ec.europa.eu/growth/tools-databases/regprof/) is more than qualification-level categories. The public application's `assets/config.json` identifies a JSON service at `https://api.tech.ec.europa.eu/regprof20/prodmigration`. Its unauthenticated `/regprofs` list exposes qualification categories and regulated activities; `/regprof/si?id=…` contains structured qualification requirements, including `education_duration_value`, `education_duration_unit`, `traineeship_duration_value`, `traineeship_duration_unit`, mandatory-traineeship and state-exam fields. Germany's basic-medical-training record 655132 reports six years and a twelve-month traineeship. Germany's own current ordinance includes the practical year within the course and gives a standard examination-inclusive duration of six years three months. Adding the database's education and traineeship fields would therefore produce a misleading total.

The live Doctor of Medicine listing returned 1,523 regulated-profession records, of which 32 exactly identified Basic medical training under Article 24. These include archived UK data. A bounded extraction fetched the screening-information endpoint for each of the 32, retaining only duration fields in ignored research scratch. Some qualification records are missing; Poland uses an OTHER unit with explanatory text. Dataset records have no verified observation year or revision date in the extracted fields. The preview can show this as a dated database snapshot with missingness and freshness limits, not as an independently verified current legal minimum in every country. The JSON interface works but is not a documented stable public API. Frontend export endpoints exist; a tested `/regprofs/export` returned JSON null, so a working bulk download was not established.

The [Commission legal notice](https://commission.europa.eu/legal-notice_en) provides default CC BY 4.0 reuse for EU-owned content, subject to exceptions. A specific licence covering all member-state database contributions and bulk extraction was not located. Only selected factual duration fields and original analytical coding are proposed for the preview; no wholesale database redistribution is authorised by this check.

A related [Commission staff working document, SWD(2026)64](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:52026SC0064), section 4.3, contains a real 27-entry GP/family-medicine training-duration table attributed to the EC survey and WONCA Europe/UEMO. This is a published table to transcribe, not an observation-level download. It reports postgraduate GP training, not total years from school. Luxembourg has a range; the printed code SL is ambiguous and must remain unassigned unless clarified. The document also reports stakeholder arguments for maintaining minimum basic medical training. Those views are not causal outcome evidence. The current legal minimum for EU basic medical training combines years and hours; national curricula can exceed it.

### WHO National Health Workforce Accounts: real indicator, medical observations not obtained

The current [NHWA indicator 2–05, Duration of education and training](https://apps.who.int/nhwaportal/Files/2%20%E2%80%93%2005%20Duration%20of%20education%20and%20training.pdf), defines years to complete each health-workforce programme's full curriculum. It excludes specialisations and optional additional certifications, is disaggregated by programme, and is intended for annual reporting. The 2017 handbook's older numbering differs; it must not be confused with the 2023 second edition.

The [NHWA portal](https://apps.who.int/nhwaportal/) could be retrieved using a public-session cookie jar, but the returned shell showed a technical-issue message and an embedded Power BI report. The shell offers PDF/PNG/PPTX presentation exports; a functioning raw medical-duration CSV/XLSX or API was not verified. A targeted search of GHO OData indicator metadata returned no corresponding duration indicator. This scoped negative result is not evidence that no NHWA medical observations exist.

Actual duration observations are demonstrated for nursing: the [State of the world's nursing 2025 report](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/381329/9789240110236-eng.pdf), figure 6.3 and section 6.3, publishes programme-duration summaries for latest observations in 2018–2023. Its body says 140 reporting countries while the figure note says 139; retain that inconsistency. A 2020 Eswatini national nursing profile also reports a three-year programme. These cannot be substituted for medical-programme observations. Country-year coverage for medicine remains unverified, so no WHO medical world distribution will appear in this preview.

[WHO data.who.int terms](https://data.who.int/about/data/terms-and-conditions) specify CC BY 4.0 for datasets on that platform, but this does not establish the licence of the separate NHWA application. [General WHO data terms](https://www.who.int/about/policies/publishing/data-policy/terms-and-conditions) and the publication's own licence must be checked for the exact product. Portal-specific raw-data reuse terms remain unresolved. The two new catalogue drafts record these limitations.

### Medicine preview produced

The selected cases are California (conventional US MD route), England (standard five-year medicine), Bavaria in Germany (federal medical course and Bavarian GP rules), Japan, India (domestic MBBS), and Singapore (NTU local undergraduate route). Bavaria was chosen because both the current nine-year Gymnasium and binding regional specialty requirements can be identified, rather than implying a single school pathway throughout Germany. NTU replaces an initially considered NUS example because the live NTU programme page was directly retrievable and specifies five years; this changes no national scope assumption.

The data package contains 50 stage records resolving to 26 source-register entries, six harmonised school baselines, 21 selected UIS programme rows, 24 World Bank observations, 32 selected EU basic-medical-training duration records and a 27-entry transcription from the separate Commission GP table. The EU distribution includes only 26 non-archived records with year-valued education duration: three report five years, 21 report six, and two report seven. Four missing records, Poland's OTHER unit and the archived UK record are excluded explicitly. Neither the EU data nor the six routes are an estimate of competence effects.

The India row is provisional: direct NMC document retrieval failed, so it preserves indexed primary excerpts and official government-provider/court corroboration, identifies the reading limit, and keeps full-original and current NExT checks open before publication. Other limits include the older Japanese English legal translation and the undated EU records. The 2026 Japanese insured-facility-manager requirement is a separate later role, potentially overlapping specialty experience, not another mandatory period added to all medical careers. California's first general licence at twelve postgraduate months is separated from three-year residency completion and the initial-renewal training condition.

The self-contained preview is generated from Markdown/YAML authoring sources and the data package, with all clocks linked to the same stages. It was registered in ReportScout. The structural single-file audit reports no blockers. A self-review of desktop and phone renders found and corrected clipped stage labels; no independent source or screenshot review is claimed.
