# A translation series beginning with neighbouring languages

**I recommend a series, starting with a small comparison of the questionnaires already downloaded.** Check three item families in Czech, Slovak and Polish first, then use corpus evidence to investigate the differences that survive a careful reading. German can join once its fielded questionnaire has been retrieved and checked. This gives the broader work a tested method without making the first snack carry every question.

There are sources for all three strands you proposed. **The strongest immediate result would be a documented account of which selected questions differ in meaning.** A change in wording alongside a change in answers could identify a problem worth investigating. It would not establish that the wording caused the response change.

**You confirmed this direction on 24 November 2026 (Tuesday): a series starting with neighbouring languages, with TIMSS as the education-survey topic.** You also added official EU translations to the potential investigations. The exact first-study sample and complete research brief still need agreement; the study shapes below remain proposals for that discussion.

## What would corpus evidence add?

**Parallel corpora can locate translation alternatives; monolingual corpora can show how each expression is used.** Both need context reading. Neither demonstrates that survey respondents understood two items equivalently.

| Resource | Access checked here | Best use and limit |
|---|---|---|
| [Czech National Corpus, InterCorp and Treq](https://wiki.korpus.cz/doku.php/en:kurz:zaciname) | Official guidance describes limited public access and free registration for full access. No authenticated query was attempted; the Treq homepage failed through the web tool. | InterCorp supports aligned comparisons; a selected SYN corpus supports Czech usage. Pin release and genre. The [InterCorp description](https://wiki.korpus.cz/doku.php/en:cnk:intercorp) distinguishes fiction core, automated collections and a Czech pivot; the English page is marked as an old revision. |
| [Sketch Engine](https://www.sketchengine.eu/corpora-and-languages/corpus-list/) | The connected catalogue returned metadata for Czech, Slovak, Polish, German and English web corpora, with Arabic and Japanese candidates too. No concordance or word-sketch query was run. | Word sketches and concordances can test competing readings. Different crawl dates, genres and tagging systems prevent treating cross-language scores as a common semantic scale. |
| [OPUS](https://opus.nlpl.eu/) | Its public collection listing and query/download routes are accessible. No corpus package was downloaded. | Select one named collection/version with the required language overlap. OPUS is a collection portal; constituent rights, domains and alignment quality differ. Exclude synthetic translations from natural-usage evidence. |
| [Tatoeba](https://tatoeba.org/en/terms_of_use) | Current terms were accessible and describe text licensing. | An openly licensed source of illustrative translated sentences, with sentence attribution. Its contributed examples cannot supply representative collocation frequencies. |
| [Leipzig Corpora Collection](https://api.wortschatz-leipzig.de/ws/swagger-ui/index.html) | API documentation was accessible. The download-terms page returned an anti-bot challenge; no bypass was attempted. | A potential monolingual fallback. The specific archive, language, genre and licence remain unverified; the API documentation's licence label alone does not clear every corpus archive. |

For a pilot, compare full expressions as well as their component words, include alternative translations, and read a reproducible sample of concordances. Separate original texts from translations where metadata permit. Record the query, corpus version, grammatical relation or window, frequency threshold and association measure. Sparse evidence should produce an unresolved judgement, rather than a claim that an expression is equivalent or absent.

## Can the same check be done in education surveys?

**Yes, there are credible starting sources.** The [PISA 2022 database](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/datasets/pisa-2022-database.html) explicitly lists both master and national questionnaires, including Czechia, Slovakia, Poland and Germany. This is the most direct international listing found for the proposed national-language comparison. It remains necessary to match item identifiers, mode, grade or target population, national additions and source changes across cycles.

For TIMSS, the [IEA repository](https://www.iea.nl/data-tools/repository/timss) provides international data and documentation by cycle and grade. The [Czech provider's 2023 page](https://www.csicr.cz/cz/Mezinarodni-setreni/TIMSS/Datove-soubory-a-dotazniky/TIMSS-2023) lists student, teacher, school and parent questionnaires. [Austria's national page](https://www.iqs.gv.at/downloads/internationale-studien/timss/timss-2023) also lists questionnaires, while warning that their generated PDF layout differs from the participant view. An indexed excerpt of the [TIMSS database guide](https://www.iea.nl/sites/default/files/2025-02/T23_UG-International-Database.pdf) describes national adaptation records; those records do not replace the complete national text. The full guide exceeded the retrieval tool's size limit and was not read. No national TIMSS file was downloaded or aligned here.

[ESS translation documentation](https://www.europeansocialsurvey.org/sites/default/files/2024-09/ESS10_Quality_Report.pdf) provides a methodological reference, including TRAPD and pretesting. Its recurring core items are candidates for a later within-programme history. [ISSP documentation](https://www.gesis.org/en/issp/data-and-documentation/module-development) includes source and national questionnaires; its archive says data download follows registration. Language and item coverage for a selected repeat module still need checking. The ESS portal and disclaimer fetches failed in this pass, so current access and exact licence text remain unresolved here.

## Three possible first snacks

These are proposed research slices, not approved work. The effort ranges are agent planning estimates, including source checking, analysis records and a draft for preview. They exclude waiting for access, specialist review and your preview decision. They are not measurements or delivery promises.

| Shape | What would be checked | What it could honestly show | Main limit and estimated effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Do neighbouring-language questionnaires ask the same thing?** | Three candidate families in Czech, Slovak and Polish EVS 2017; add the Czech/Slovak WVS contrast as a clearly separate comparison. Use a focused corpus check for one disputed expression. | Which selected fielded items preserve or change the proposition, conditions, force or answer task; what corpus usage adds to those readings. | Selected cases cannot estimate a general error rate. Language judgements need review before a strong public verdict. Allow roughly one to two agent working days, conditional on access. This is the recommended start of a series. |
| **Does an item change meaning between waves?** | Two families in two languages across up to three waves of one programme, conditional on questionnaire availability. Align source and translations before inspecting response trends. | A documented translation history, followed by descriptive response changes with weighted uncertainty and administration notes. | Changes may coincide without being causal. If earlier fielded texts cannot be established, stop at a documented availability gap. Allow roughly two to four agent working days. |
| **Do education questionnaires preserve the same meaning?** | Two recurring context-item families in three languages across two TIMSS cycles, conditional on matching sources. PISA remains an alternative source if useful. | Whether apparently comparable education attitudes or experiences are asked equivalently, including national adaptations. | Grade, age, mode and cycle changes need explicit treatment; rights for excerpts must be resolved. This first slice would not audit achievement test items or explain country score differences. Allow roughly two to four agent working days. |

The corpus strand fits naturally after the neighbouring-language audit establishes a real linguistic question. A later distant-language extension can then ask whether the same proposed distinction survives different lexical systems. Choosing Arabic or Japanese now merely because the corpus catalogue lists them would get ahead of your research question and available language expertise.
