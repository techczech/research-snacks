# Author decisions and open interview

## Current research brief for confirmation

**Question:** Do the equivalent key words chosen in Czech, Slovak, Polish and German survey questionnaires carry comparable associations in ordinary written use? The outcome is a linguistic judgement supported by corpus evidence. Whole questions identify context; the first snack does not estimate effects on survey answers or rank national values.

**Confirmed by the author:** a series beginning with neighbouring languages; lexical associations as the first focus; judgement with corpus support; Czech, Slovak, Polish and German; three word groups covering industriousness/hard work, responsibility and duty/obligation. TIMSS and official EU translations remain later investigations alongside more distant languages and consistency between waves.

**Source sample:** EVS 2017 national questionnaires for the same selected child-quality and social-duty contexts. Germany's CAPI questionnaire is the main German source; the selected lexical forms were also located in its four web/postal variants. The checked German terms are `Fleiß`, `Verantwortungsgefühl`, `Pflicht` for having children and `Verpflichtung` for work. The latter distinction must be preserved, not collapsed into one presumed equivalent. The existing Czech/Slovak WVS hard-work contrast is additional lexical context only. No new WVS material is needed. Exact form/item/page provenance is in the [sample proposal](proposal-neighbouring-language-keywords.md) and [German source receipt](evidence-german-questionnaire-wording.md).

**Corpora:** use Czech Web 2023 `preloaded/cstenten23_mj2`, Slovak Web 2023 `preloaded/sktenten23_rft1`, Polish Web 2019 `preloaded/pltenten19_rft3` and German Web 2023 `preloaded/detenten23_rft4`. Each exact metadata request succeeded. The German rft4 annotation update uses the same source texts as rft3 according to the provider; its Wikipedia component dates to 2024. Record the crawl periods, pipelines and genre limits. InterCorp is supporting evidence if existing access and suitable shared texts permit it. No new registration, subscription or installation is required to begin the main pilot.

**Proposed method:** first record the exact questionnaire forms and an agent-proposed alternative set, distinguishing them from established equivalents. Query the forms/lemmas and multiword expressions, collect within-language collocational profiles, and inspect a reproducible pilot sample of up to 30 concordance passages per candidate with document/domain spread recorded. Expand an ambiguous candidate to at most 60 passages before leaving it unresolved; these are practical pilot bounds, not statistical power claims. Keep contrary examples. Record actual queries, corpus versions, sampling rules, excluded/duplicate lines and short evidence references. Word-sketch grammatical relations and association scores remain within-corpus aids, not a shared cross-language scale.

**Judgements:** assess plausible associations and overlapping senses in context, with provisional categories such as personal virtue, effort, physical toil, moral obligation, legal requirement and accountability. These are open interpretation prompts, not a keyword classifier. Record confidence and abstention. The recommended allocation is that the agent assigns provisional readings and the author inspects representative, contradictory and uncertain cases. This is not independent native-speaker validation. The author has not yet agreed to that role.

**Interpretation:** compare the evidence within each language first, then judge whether the candidate equivalents show similar associations, different emphases, or insufficient evidence. Distinguish chosen words from proposed alternatives and dictionary glosses. Similar spelling is not proof of equivalence; corpus usage does not establish respondent understanding, all speakers' intuitions or spoken usage. No author prediction has been supplied; do not invent one or frame a result as confirming a prior belief.

**Bounded exploration:** allow up to 30 minutes for query/access feasibility after brief confirmation, then up to four agent working hours for one lexical group as the first evidence slice. If method or access fails, report the actual limit and adjust before expanding. The remaining groups follow after that slice is inspectable, within the earlier one-to-two-agent-day planning estimate. No additional spending, survey recruitment or external reviewer is authorised. All time estimates are planning estimates; actual times and tokens are measured from run records.

**Deliverable and rights:** a first evidence slice, then a draft snack preview with the four-language word table, contextual examples, corpus evidence, judgement provenance and limits. Keep restricted originals, full questionnaire extracts, account metadata and raw service responses outside public commits. Record provider terms without inventing stricter clauses. Share only permitted derived outputs and short illustrative quotations. No first-person claims in the author's name. No production publication before the author has looked at the preview.

## Potential investigations in the series

1. **Neighbouring-language questionnaire key words come first.** Investigate industriousness/hard work, responsibility and duty/obligation in Czech, Slovak, Polish and German, through linguistic judgement with corpus support. Whole questions supply context; the first snack is not a whole-question equivalence audit or response-effect study. The detailed method and judgement role await full-brief confirmation.
2. **Corpus collocations in more distant languages remain a proposed extension.** Investigate whether candidate equivalents carry comparable associations and uses.
3. **Consistency across survey iterations remains a proposed extension.** Separate translation changes from source-item changes and examine any response shifts descriptively.
4. **TIMSS questionnaire translations are a confirmed topic of interest.** PISA, ESS and ISSP remain possible comparison sources rather than substitutes already selected by the author.
5. **Official EU translations are a new potential investigation requested on 24 November.** Compare how selected terms or expressions are officially rendered in different languages and how the renderings differ. A possible method is to align the same passage and document version across languages, then compare institutional usage with ordinary corpus usage. Specific institutions, document types, terms, languages, sources and access/licensing have not been selected or checked. Record this as a future candidate, not an authorised research run.

## Writing declaration

The scoping report is FOR DOMINIK. Its purpose is to support choosing a first study, not to establish translation errors. The reader knows the Czech culture check raised wording concerns; they need accessible sources, honest conclusions, limits and feasible alternatives. Private culture-guide content, account details, personal paths and research respondent data are irrelevant and excluded. The genre requires a clear recommendation, source map, proposed study shapes, unresolved questions and measured usage coverage.

The domain map supplies terms and risks. The source/access section establishes feasibility. The study-shape section explains what each option answers and cannot answer. The decision section asks for scope only; it does not turn a provisional recommendation into author approval.
