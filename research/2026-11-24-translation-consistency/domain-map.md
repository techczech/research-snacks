## Terms and competing interpretations

- **Literal similarity** concerns words and syntax. It does not establish that respondents answer the same question.
- **Semantic equivalence** concerns the proposition, referent, conditions, intensity and presuppositions expressed by the whole item. Different-looking translations can preserve these; cognates can conceal differences.
- **Measurement equivalence** concerns how an item functions for respondents. A linguistic audit identifies risks, while respondent research or statistical modelling addresses measurement.
- **Collocation** concerns words occurring together in a specified corpus and grammatical relation or window. A corpus pattern is evidence about language use in that collection, rather than direct evidence about survey respondents' understanding.
- **Translation change**, **source-item change**, **national adaptation**, **response-format change** and **coding/harmonisation change** are separate events. A joint dataset label is not a substitute for the fielded instrument.

## Positions and methods

The [ESS round 10 quality report](https://www.europeansocialsurvey.org/sites/default/files/2024-09/ESS10_Quality_Report.pdf) describes team translation, review, adjudication, pretesting and documentation (TRAPD), alongside verification and other quality checks. The [EVS 2017 methodological guidelines](https://access.gesis.org/dbk/65214) distinguish reuse of existing translations from correction of serious errors. These approaches seek equivalent instruments while retaining documented decisions; a changed translation need not be an accidental inconsistency.

A first audit should align the English source item, national question, response options, instructions and showcard. Record the survey programme, wave, actual fieldwork year, language, mode, questionnaire version, page and item identifier separately. Judge meaning at item level, retaining an uncertain category and short evidence excerpts. Exact-string comparison may find edits; it cannot decide equivalence.

Corpus work should then examine the selected expressions in context. Use aligned passages to locate translation alternatives and original-language monolingual passages to test their ordinary usage. Record corpus release, genre, original language, alignment quality, lemma/token queries, grammatical relation or window, minimum frequency and association measure. Concordance reading is necessary before interpreting a collocation list. Cross-language raw counts or association scores alone do not measure equivalence.

A wave comparison should hold the programme and item lineage explicit, including changes in the English source. Response changes are descriptive associations unless a suitable experimental or quasi-experimental design separates wording from period, population and administration effects. A repeated cross-section is not a panel.

There is no universal sample size or reliability coefficient for this proposed audit. A pilot of three item families in three neighbouring languages is a proposed convenience sample, selected because earlier work raised questions. It cannot estimate a survey-wide translation-error rate. Annotation agreement, sampling needs and any inferential model must be set after the author chooses the question and languages; none has been measured here.

[InterCorp](https://intercorp.korpus.cz/?lang=en), [KonText](https://wiki.korpus.cz/doku.php/en:manualy:kontext:novy_dotaz) and [Treq](https://wiki.korpus.cz/doku.php/en:kurz:zaciname) provide parallel-corpus and Czech-corpus routes. The English overview was marked as an old revision, so this scoping does not name its release as current. The Treq homepage could not be retrieved through the web tool; no authenticated CNC query was attempted.

Sketch Engine's connected corpus catalogue responded. It lists Czech, Slovak, Polish, German and English web corpora, plus Arabic and Japanese candidates for a later extension. Metadata availability does not prove a particular concordance or word-sketch query will succeed. No linguistic queries were run. [Service terms](https://www.sketchengine.eu/terms-of-use/) and each selected corpus's rights remain separate.

[OPUS](https://opus.nlpl.eu/) supplies downloadable parallel collections and an open query route. Choose a named constituent and version, inspect its language overlap and rights, and exclude synthetic translations from evidence about independent natural usage. [Tatoeba](https://tatoeba.org/en/terms_of_use) is a possible openly licensed illustration source, but contributed example sentences do not supply representative collocation frequencies. The [Leipzig API](https://api.wortschatz-leipzig.de/ws/swagger-ui/index.html) is a monolingual alternative; its [download terms](https://wortschatz-leipzig.de/en/usage) need checking against the chosen archive.

## Education and other surveys

[TIMSS](https://www.iea.nl/data-tools/repository/timss) has international data/documentation by cycle and grade. The [Czech national provider](https://www.csicr.cz/cz/Mezinarodni-setreni/TIMSS/Datove-soubory-a-dotazniky/TIMSS-2023) lists national 2023 questionnaires. [Austria](https://www.iqs.gv.at/downloads/internationale-studien/timss/timss-2023) provides a further national route. The TIMSS 2023 database guide describes a National Adaptations Database; an adaptation record must not be mistaken for a full fielded translation. No national TIMSS questionnaire was downloaded or compared here.

The [PISA 2022 database page](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/datasets/pisa-2022-database.html) explicitly lists master and national questionnaires, including Czechia, Slovakia, Poland, Germany and more distant languages. It is a strong alternative for an education translation audit, conditional on item and mode matching. The [ESS](https://www.europeansocialsurvey.org/) and [ISSP](https://www.gesis.org/en/issp/data-and-documentation/module-development) offer recurring social-survey items and national documentation. Detailed availability of selected languages and repeated items still needs checking.

## Traps and useful gaps

Earlier working notes flag industriousness versus hard work, responsibility versus obligation, and conditional versus general care for parents. These are candidate cases to re-read in the original instruments, not findings of this scoping. The same notes corrected an earlier interpretation of the working-mother item: a preschool qualifier in a harmonised label was absent from both checked Czech questionnaires. That is a warning about relying on labels.

Differences in responses may reflect selection limits, scale points, question order, fieldwork mode, sample design, period events or weighting. Stable wording does not guarantee stable interpretation. Uniform subgroup gaps do not identify a wording effect. Corpus translationese, genre imbalance, sparse distant-language coverage and shared-source dependence can all create misleading apparent similarity.

A small documented audit could establish exactly which selected fielded texts differ and why those differences merit further study. It could not establish that national values differ only because of translation, that one rendering is universally correct, or that a changed phrase caused an observed answer shift.
